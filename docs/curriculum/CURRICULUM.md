# Canonical options learning system — P01 design v1.0.0

As of 22 September 2026. This is a research and curriculum-design release, not a published course or an application design. The canonical system retains 67 original modules and 1,019 original topics, adds three entry bridges and three research-driven topics, and gives every module an individual learning dossier. There are **zero fully authored, reviewed, publication-ready lessons** in this release. Remaining lesson authoring belongs to P17; P02 decides the release contract.

The normalized hierarchy is program → phase → module → topic → addressable scope unit. Chapters are omitted because these modules do not need an additional navigation layer. Each original subtopic scope remains one coherent, independently addressable unit with an `.S01` ID; punctuation was not treated as a semantic parser. P17 may split a broad unit into narrower teaching units only with successor mappings. This preserves meaning while making the remaining granularity work visible.

The program has five phases: entry and safety; contracts and decision foundations; pricing, mathematics and volatility; research, risk and professional practice; specialist branches. Phases organize learning; hard competency prerequisites determine entry. An experienced learner may demonstrate a prerequisite by diagnostic work. A beginner sees premium, loss, sizing and assignment before stochastic calculus. Basic coding precedes every coding-dependent project.

All module dossiers are linked in [MODULE_INDEX.md](MODULE_INDEX.md). They contain why the module matters, measurable outcomes, ordered original scope, mathematical/programming requirements, reading assignments with access limitations, distinct conceptual and numerical exercises, the preserved practical assessment, mistakes, checkpoints, mastery criteria and remediation. Machine exports carry the same designs in [modules.json](../../data/v1/modules.json).

The seven paths share these modules rather than duplicating lessons. [PATHS.md](PATHS.md) lists their exact prerequisite-complete sequences, audiences, entry criteria, branches, exits, hours and gates. The paths are Absolute Beginner, Retail Options Trader, Quantitative Options Trader, Volatility Trader, Market-Making, Indian Options Trader and Systematic Options Trader. The seven original specialization branches remain separate archival/curricular records, including portfolio hedging and cross-asset/OTC work.

M15–M20 remain accessible as an optional chart/indicator specialization. They no longer delay the general options path. Every indicator needs an objective formula and initialization, timing, parameter sensitivity, costed baseline and incremental-information test. This is a design recommendation based on the difference between pattern description and demonstrated trading value, not a claim that all technical analysis is ineffective. The Lo–Mamaysky–Wang work is a methodological reading candidate, not universal validation of named patterns. See CL16 and [research methodology](../research/RESEARCH_METHODOLOGY.md).

The practice sequence moves from signed cash-flow tables to synthetic numerical work, historical reconstruction, paper positions, lifecycle simulation and statistical evaluation. Real-money trading is neither required nor accepted as proof of mastery. The twelve projects develop distinct capabilities; seven capstones integrate them. Successful rejection of an invalid strategy is a successful research outcome.

Recommended first publication slice, subject to P02: units/returns; option rights and premium; intrinsic/extrinsic value; multipliers; exercise versus settlement; long/short terminal losses; covered-call downside; vertical cash paths; costs and conservative sizing; a reconciliation drill. Use roughly 24–36 carefully authored topics drawn from M01/B01/M21/M22/M24/M25/M45/M49, with synthetic contracts and an explicit distinction between a hypothetical contract and a current exchange rule. Selection is provisional: none is marked ready merely by inclusion here. This slice gives a learner a safe, assessable foundation and does not depend on licensing a historical options database.

Estimate 180–360 editorial hours for that first slice, including lesson writing, original diagrams/tables, worked examples, fresh assessment variants, accessibility and a separate review. For the remaining encyclopedia, allow roughly 6–14 hours per ordinary topic and 12–30 per advanced topic, plus integration and corrections; the itemized queue totals 9,990–17,202 hours before a 15–25% integration allowance (roughly 11,500–21,500 hours overall), to be revised after the first batch. Study-hour estimates measure learner effort and are separate from authoring hours. No schedule or proficiency guarantee follows from these estimates.
