# M06 — Financial statements and equity fundamentals

Status: learning design; topic lessons remain P17. Slug: `financial-statements-and-equity-fundamentals`. Intermediate; 14–36 hours. Tags: fundamental-analysis, fundamentals-macro-and-events.

Translate balance-sheet and cash-flow surprises into explicit underlying scenarios.

Hard prerequisites: C-M02. Recommended preparation: C-M01. Optional enrichment: none. Diagnostic: Why can a profitable firm run out of cash? Bridge: B01.

Estimation basis: Editorial estimate for selected readings, scope navigation, one worked calculation, one transfer variant, practical work and remediation; 45–90 minutes per ordinary scope topic plus 2–8 practical hours, longer for specialist derivation. Projects budgeted separately. Not measured learner time.

Objectives:

- Translate balance-sheet and cash-flow surprises into explicit underlying scenarios.
- Solve and explain: Revenue 100, expense 80, receivables increase 30: simplified operating cash flow -10 despite profit 20.
- Audit the failure mode: Substituting valuation conviction for an option timing thesis.

Mathematics: A (codes in MATH_ROADMAP). Programming: None for core explanations; optional Python for replication.

Reading: R17:balance sheet, income and cash-flow sections. Resource verification limits control use; a TOC assignment is a candidate, not a reviewed chapter. All learners can complete the synthetic numerical case without buying a book.

Ordered scope:

- M06.01 — Income statement: revenue, gross profit, operating income, net income, EPS
- M06.02 — Balance sheet: assets, liabilities, equity, working capital, debt structure
- M06.03 — Cash flow statement: operating, investing, financing; earnings versus cash generation
- M06.04 — Accounting quality: accruals, one-offs, stock compensation, capitalized costs
- M06.05 — Growth and margins: revenue drivers, operating leverage, unit economics
- M06.06 — Valuation multiples: P/E, P/S, EV/EBITDA, free-cash-flow yield; comparability limits
- M06.07 — Discounted cash flow: discount rates, terminal value, sensitivity to assumptions
- M06.08 — Capital structure: dilution, convertibles, refinancing, bankruptcy and recovery
- M06.09 — Dividends and buybacks: declaration, ex-date, payment, authorization versus execution
- M06.10 — Consensus and expectations: estimate revisions, guidance, whisper expectations, surprise definitions
- M06.11 — Earnings quality: reported versus adjusted metrics, footnotes, management incentives
- M06.12 — Industry context: cyclicality, competition, regulation, sector-specific operating metrics
- M06.13 — Catalyst identification: earnings calls, filings, guidance, capital raises, legal outcomes
- M06.14 — Equity-credit linkage: distress, jump-to-default, structural credit models and put skew

Teaching brief for module delivery: begin with the checkpoint dilemma, elicit the learner's units and obligations, work the numerical case, then use the practical assignment to challenge the main assumption. Ask for a counterexample before extending the scope. This is a module delivery plan, not a topic lesson.

Practice:

**EX-M06-C** Why can a profitable firm run out of cash?

Reference: Use the module objective and stated failure mode; credit explicit conditions and a counterexample, not confidence.

**EX-M06-N** Compute, show units and assumptions; then change one input and justify the direction. Revenue 100, expense 80, receivables increase 30: simplified operating cash flow -10 despite profit 20.

Reference: Revenue 100, expense 80, receivables increase 30: simplified operating cash flow -10 despite profit 20.

**EX-M06-P** Read one company filing and produce a one-page earnings-risk brief.

Reference: Separate reported facts, adjusted measures, expectations and unsupported narrative.

Common mistakes:

- Substituting valuation conviction for an option timing thesis.
- Accepting a result without its units, assumptions or source date.

Checkpoint questions:

- Why can a profitable firm run out of cash?
- Which assumption in your numerical result is most vulnerable, and what changes if it fails?

Mastery:

- Separate reported facts, adjusted measures, expectations and unsupported narrative.
- At least 85% on quiz and rubric; no unresolved critical errors; solve a fresh transfer variant with reasoning.

Remediation: Diagnose the mistaken unit, model, observation or cash-flow step; revisit the cited entry bridge or source section, annotate the correction, then attempt an unseen variant after a separate review session.
