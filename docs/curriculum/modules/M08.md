# M08 — Intermarket relationships and correlation regimes

Status: learning design; topic lessons remain P17. Slug: `intermarket-relationships-and-correlation-regimes`. Intermediate; 12–32 hours. Tags: macro, fundamentals-macro-and-events.

Quantify changing factor and correlation exposures across assets.

Hard prerequisites: C-M10. Recommended preparation: C-M07. Optional enrichment: none. Diagnostic: Can a historically negative hedge correlation be assumed in a crisis? Bridge: B02.

Estimation basis: Editorial estimate for selected readings, scope navigation, one worked calculation, one transfer variant, practical work and remediation; 45–90 minutes per ordinary scope topic plus 2–8 practical hours, longer for specialist derivation. Projects budgeted separately. Not measured learner time.

Objectives:

- Quantify changing factor and correlation exposures across assets.
- Solve and explain: Weights .5/.5, vols 20%/30%, correlation 0: portfolio vol 18.03%; correlation 1:25%.
- Audit the failure mode: Treating sample correlation as stable causation.

Mathematics: S, L (codes in MATH_ROADMAP). Programming: None for core explanations; optional Python for replication.

Reading: R72:metadata only; R21:covariance sections. Resource verification limits control use; a TOC assignment is a candidate, not a reviewed chapter. All learners can complete the synthetic numerical case without buying a book.

Ordered scope:

- M08.01 — Equity-bond relationships: changing inflation and growth exposures
- M08.02 — Price-yield relationship: duration, convexity and rate shocks
- M08.03 — Dollar and global assets: trade-weighted indices, funding currencies, currency translation
- M08.04 — Gold: real rates, currency, positioning and safe-haven narratives
- M08.05 — Oil: supply, demand, geopolitics, inventories and inflation channels
- M08.06 — Credit-equity-volatility links: leverage, distress, risk aversion and liquidity
- M08.07 — Sector rotations: rate sensitivity, cyclicality, index concentration
- M08.08 — Correlation versus causation: common factors, conditional correlation and changing samples
- M08.09 — Tail dependence: diversification during stress, asymmetric correlations, copulas
- M08.10 — Lead-lag relationships: timing alignment, nonsynchronous markets, spurious predictability
- M08.11 — Cross-market confirmation: hypothesis, horizon, invalidation and transaction costs
- M08.12 — Currency-adjusted portfolios: local versus base-currency returns and hedge ratios

Common mistakes:

- Treating sample correlation as stable causation.
- Accepting a result without its units, assumptions or source date.

Checkpoint questions:

- Can a historically negative hedge correlation be assumed in a crisis?
