# M45 — Trade risk, sizing and survival

Status: learning design; topic lessons remain P17. Slug: `trade-risk-sizing-and-survival`. Beginner; 17–42 hours. Tags: risk, risk-and-portfolio-construction.

Size against stress loss, drawdown and funding uncertainty rather than confidence.

Hard prerequisites: C-M10, C-B01. Recommended preparation: C-M05, C-M21. Optional enrichment: none. Diagnostic: When should a noisy positive Kelly estimate still be rejected? Bridge: B01.

Estimation basis: Editorial estimate for selected readings, scope navigation, one worked calculation, one transfer variant, practical work and remediation; 45–90 minutes per ordinary scope topic plus 2–8 practical hours, longer for specialist derivation. Projects budgeted separately. Not measured learner time.

Objectives:

- Size against stress loss, drawdown and funding uncertainty rather than confidence.
- Solve and explain: Bankroll 10000, training stress budget 1%, stressed loss 250/lot: floor 100/250=0 lots.
- Audit the failure mode: Sizing from margin; full Kelly with uncertain tails.

Mathematics: A, S (codes in MATH_ROADMAP). Programming: None for core explanations; optional Python for replication.

Reading: R73:original information-rate paper metadata; R01:X. Resource verification limits control use; a TOC assignment is a candidate, not a reviewed chapter. All learners can complete the synthetic numerical case without buying a book.

Ordered scope:

- M45.01 — Risk definition: premium, terminal maximum loss, stress loss and cash obligation
- M45.02 — Risk per trade: account equity, risk budget, minimum contract size and rounding
- M45.03 — Notional exposure: gross/net, leverage, delta-adjusted and tail exposure
- M45.04 — Expected value: full outcome distribution, costs and uncertainty in probabilities
- M45.05 — Win rate and payoff ratio: average win/loss, rare losses and misleading hit rates
- M45.06 — Probability of profit: horizon, model, physical/pricing measure and costs
- M45.07 — Risk of ruin: absorbing barriers, dependence, changing edge and finite capital
- M45.08 — Drawdown: peak-to-trough loss, time underwater and recovery requirement
- M45.09 — Kelly criterion: expected log growth, assumptions, leverage and estimation error
- M45.10 — Fractional Kelly: model uncertainty, drawdown tolerance and practical constraints
- M45.11 — Sizing methods: fixed fraction, fixed risk, volatility scaling and scenario-based sizing
- M45.12 — Concentration: underlying, sector, expiry, event, strategy and shared risk factors
- M45.13 — Loss limits: daily/weekly limits, drawdown throttles, pause and review rules
- M45.14 — Scaling in/out: precommitted rules, changing average cost and total risk
- M45.15 — Gap and tail exposure: stops versus actual executable loss
- M45.16 — Capital allocation versus margin: economic risk first, broker requirements as another constraint
- M45.17 — Parameter uncertainty buffers: uncertain edge, heavy tails and robust size bounds

Teaching brief for module delivery: begin with the checkpoint dilemma, elicit the learner's units and obligations, work the numerical case, then use the practical assignment to challenge the main assumption. Ask for a counterexample before extending the scope. This is a module delivery plan, not a topic lesson.

Common mistakes:

- Sizing from margin; full Kelly with uncertain tails.
- Accepting a result without its units, assumptions or source date.

Checkpoint questions:

- When should a noisy positive Kelly estimate still be rejected?
- Which assumption in your numerical result is most vulnerable, and what changes if it fails?

Mastery:

- Keep loss/cash needs within a declared budget and reject infeasible minimum contract sizes.
- At least 85% on quiz and rubric; no unresolved critical errors; solve a fresh transfer variant with reasoning.

Remediation: Diagnose the mistaken unit, model, observation or cash-flow step; revisit the cited entry bridge or source section, annotate the correction, then attempt an unseen variant after a separate review session.
