# M55 — Research design, edge and hypothesis validation

Status: learning design; topic lessons remain P17. Slug: `research-design-edge-and-hypothesis-validation`. Intermediate; 15–38 hours. Tags: quantitative, research-data-and-software.

Write falsifiable hypotheses and isolate selection from final evaluation.

Hard prerequisites: C-M11, C-M54. Recommended preparation: C-M12. Optional enrichment: none. Diagnostic: What result would cause abandonment of this strategy? Bridge: B03.

Estimation basis: Editorial estimate for selected readings, scope navigation, one worked calculation, one transfer variant, practical work and remediation; 45–90 minutes per ordinary scope topic plus 2–8 practical hours, longer for specialist derivation. Projects budgeted separately. Not measured learner time.

Objectives:

- Write falsifiable hypotheses and isolate selection from final evaluation.
- Solve and explain: 20 independent tests at 5% have probability 1-.95^20=64.15% of at leastone false rejection under globalnull.
- Audit the failure mode: Testing until significant; failure to report all trials.

Mathematics: S, N (codes in MATH_ROADMAP). Programming: B03 plus arrays, functions and tests.

Reading: R23:sections 1–3; R53:abstract/definition scope. Resource verification limits control use; a TOC assignment is a candidate, not a reviewed chapter. All learners can complete the synthetic numerical case without buying a book.

Ordered scope:

- M55.01 — Strategy versus setup versus signal: economic rule, market configuration and trigger
- M55.02 — Trade versus risk management versus portfolio management: execution instance and distinct decision layers
- M55.03 — Economic rationale: who pays, why, persistence, constraints and competing explanations
- M55.04 — Testable hypotheses: precise observables, forecasts, horizon, costs and invalidation
- M55.05 — Alpha and beta: benchmark, factor exposure, risk premium and residual return
- M55.06 — Signal quality: predictive distribution, calibration, information coefficient and decay
- M55.07 — Selection bias: choosing markets, dates, parameters and success stories after seeing results
- M55.08 — Look-ahead bias: revised data, centered indicators and future-dependent labels
- M55.09 — Data snooping: repeated trials, multiple testing and hidden unsuccessful experiments
- M55.10 — Backtest overfitting: trial count, performance inflation, PBO and limitations
- M55.11 — Deflated/probabilistic Sharpe: selection, non-normality, sampling uncertainty and assumptions
- M55.12 — Robustness: neighboring parameters, subperiods, instruments, costs and structural changes
- M55.13 — Falsification: negative controls, placebo events and simpler benchmark explanations
- M55.14 — Research governance: hypothesis registry, experiment logs, replication and rejection criteria
- M55.15 — Capacity and decay: crowding, impact, implementation constraints and changing edge

Teaching brief for module delivery: begin with the checkpoint dilemma, elicit the learner's units and obligations, work the numerical case, then use the practical assignment to challenge the main assumption. Ask for a counterexample before extending the scope. This is a module delivery plan, not a topic lesson.

Practice:

**EX-M55-C** What result would cause abandonment of this strategy?

Reference: Use the module objective and stated failure mode; credit explicit conditions and a counterexample, not confidence.

**EX-M55-N** Compute, show units and assumptions; then change one input and justify the direction. 20 independent tests at 5% have probability 1-.95^20=64.15% of at leastone false rejection under globalnull.

Reference: 20 independent tests at 5% have probability 1-.95^20=64.15% of at leastone false rejection under globalnull.

**EX-M55-P** Write a preregistered hypothesis and attempt to falsify it.

Reference: Document rationale, null, tests, search count, costs and rejection criteria before results.

Common mistakes:

- Testing until significant; failure to report all trials.
- Accepting a result without its units, assumptions or source date.

Checkpoint questions:

- What result would cause abandonment of this strategy?
- Which assumption in your numerical result is most vulnerable, and what changes if it fails?

Mastery:

- Document rationale, null, tests, search count, costs and rejection criteria before results.
- At least 85% on quiz and rubric; no unresolved critical errors; solve a fresh transfer variant with reasoning.

Remediation: Diagnose the mistaken unit, model, observation or cash-flow step; revisit the cited entry bridge or source section, annotate the correction, then attempt an unseen variant after a separate review session.
