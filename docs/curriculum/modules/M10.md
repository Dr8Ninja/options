# M10 — Probability and distributions

Status: learning design; topic lessons remain P17. Slug: `probability-and-distributions`. Beginner; 16–40 hours. Tags: mathematics, mathematics-and-inference.

Compute conditional expected payoff and distinguish pricing from forecasting probabilities.

Hard prerequisites: C-M01. Recommended preparation: none. Optional enrichment: none. Diagnostic: Does an 80% win rate imply positive expectancy? Bridge: B01.

Estimation basis: Editorial estimate for selected readings, scope navigation, one worked calculation, one transfer variant, practical work and remediation; 45–90 minutes per ordinary scope topic plus 2–8 practical hours, longer for specialist derivation. Projects budgeted separately. Not measured learner time.

Objectives:

- Compute conditional expected payoff and distinguish pricing from forecasting probabilities.
- Solve and explain: 80% wins 1 and 20% loses 5: expected result -0.2 before costs.
- Audit the failure mode: Gambler's fallacy; confusing mutually exclusive and independent events.

Mathematics: A, S (codes in MATH_ROADMAP). Programming: None for core explanations; optional Python for replication.

Reading: R21:conditional probability and expectation. Resource verification limits control use; a TOC assignment is a candidate, not a reviewed chapter. All learners can complete the synthetic numerical case without buying a book.

Ordered scope:

- M10.01 — Arithmetic and algebra: equations, inequalities, exponents, logarithms, functions
- M10.02 — Probability spaces: outcomes, events, complements, unions, intersections
- M10.03 — Conditional probability: independence, dependence, joint and marginal probabilities
- M10.04 — Bayes theorem: prior, likelihood, posterior, base rates and updating
- M10.05 — Random variables: discrete and continuous, probability mass/density, cumulative distribution
- M10.06 — Expected value: weighted payoffs, linearity, conditional expectation
- M10.07 — Variance and standard deviation: dispersion, units and aggregation
- M10.08 — Covariance and correlation: Pearson, rank measures, dependence limitations
- M10.09 — Distribution families: Bernoulli, binomial, Poisson, normal, lognormal, Student-t
- M10.10 — Distribution shape: skewness, kurtosis, fat tails, percentiles and quantiles
- M10.11 — Laws of large numbers and central limit theorem: assumptions and finite-sample limits
- M10.12 — Joint distributions: multivariate outcomes, dependence, conditional tails
- M10.13 — Expected utility: risk aversion, certainty equivalents, utility versus expected wealth
- M10.14 — Jensen inequality: convexity, nonlinear expectation, variance versus volatility
- M10.15 — Physical versus risk-neutral probability: forecasting versus pricing distributions
- M10.16 — Stopping times and barriers: probability of touch, finish probability and path dependence

Common mistakes:

- Gambler's fallacy; confusing mutually exclusive and independent events.
- Accepting a result without its units, assumptions or source date.

Checkpoint questions:
