# M44 — Exotics, OTC contracts and structured optionality

Status: learning design; topic lessons remain P17. Slug: `exotics-otc-contracts-and-structured-optionality`. Advanced; 30–57 hours. Tags: specialist-derivatives, futures-and-cross-asset-derivatives.

Explain path dependence, discontinuities and embedded funding in exotics/structured products.

Hard prerequisites: C-M31, C-M14. Recommended preparation: C-M40, C-M43. Optional enrichment: none. Diagnostic: Why can equal terminal prices produce different Asian or barrier payouts? Bridge: B02.

Estimation basis: Editorial estimate for selected readings, scope navigation, one worked calculation, one transfer variant, practical work and remediation; 45–90 minutes per ordinary scope topic plus 2–8 practical hours, longer for specialist derivation. Projects budgeted separately. Not measured learner time.

Objectives:

- Explain path dependence, discontinuities and embedded funding in exotics/structured products.
- Solve and explain: Asian callK100 onfixings 90,100,110 pays 0; terminal vanilla at 110 pays 10.
- Audit the failure mode: Applying vanilla Greeks to barriers/autocallables without jump/monitoring risk.

Mathematics: C, S, N, I (codes in MATH_ROADMAP). Programming: None for core explanations; optional Python for replication.

Reading: RX08:Haug chapters 4–5/8 TOC; RX05:binary risks TOC. Resource verification limits control use; a TOC assignment is a candidate, not a reviewed chapter. All learners can complete the synthetic numerical case without buying a book.

Ordered scope:

- M44.01 — Barrier options: knock-in/out, monitoring, rebates, gaps and discontinuous hedge risk
- M44.02 — Asian options: arithmetic/geometric averages, fixing schedules and path dependence
- M44.03 — Lookback options: running extrema, sampling and pathwise pricing
- M44.04 — Bermudan options: exercise schedules, regression/tree pricing and exercise policy
- M44.05 — Digital and one-touch options: discontinuities, replication limits and settlement triggers
- M44.06 — Forward-start and cliquet options: reset exposure, forward smile and volatility dynamics
- M44.07 — Basket and rainbow options: joint distribution, correlation, worst-of/best-of exposure
- M44.08 — Autocallables: coupons, barriers, issuer call features and concentrated short convexity
- M44.09 — Variance and volatility swaps: realized definitions, caps, sampling and disruption terms
- M44.10 — Dividend swaps and options: forecasts, distributions and corporate-action risk
- M44.11 — Real options: investment timing, abandonment and nontraded underlying limitations
- M44.12 — OTC documentation: ISDA, confirmation, CSA, netting and dispute procedures
- M44.13 — Counterparty exposure: expected exposure, PFE, wrong-way risk and collateral
- M44.14 — XVA: CVA, DVA, FVA, MVA and KVA; institution-specific conventions and overlap
- M44.15 — Model and legal interaction: disruption events, fallback values and valuation disputes

Teaching brief for module delivery: begin with the checkpoint dilemma, elicit the learner's units and obligations, work the numerical case, then use the practical assignment to challenge the main assumption. Ask for a counterexample before extending the scope. This is a module delivery plan, not a topic lesson.

Practice:

**EX-M44-C** Why can equal terminal prices produce different Asian or barrier payouts?

Reference: Use the module objective and stated failure mode; credit explicit conditions and a counterexample, not confidence.

**EX-M44-N** Compute, show units and assumptions; then change one input and justify the direction. Asian callK100 onfixings 90,100,110 pays 0; terminal vanilla at 110 pays 10.

Reference: Asian callK100 onfixings 90,100,110 pays 0; terminal vanilla at 110 pays 10.

**EX-M44-P** Decompose an exotic/structured product and read a sample term sheet.

Reference: Identify path dependence, exercise, model, counterparty and documentation risks.

Common mistakes:

- Applying vanilla Greeks to barriers/autocallables without jump/monitoring risk.
- Accepting a result without its units, assumptions or source date.

Checkpoint questions:

- Why can equal terminal prices produce different Asian or barrier payouts?
- Which assumption in your numerical result is most vulnerable, and what changes if it fails?

Mastery:

- Identify path dependence, exercise, model, counterparty and documentation risks.

Remediation: Diagnose the mistaken unit, model, observation or cash-flow step; revisit the cited entry bridge or source section, annotate the correction, then attempt an unseen variant after a separate review session.
