# M21 — Option fundamentals and contract literacy

Status: learning design; topic lessons remain P17. Slug: `option-fundamentals-and-contract-literacy`. Beginner; 17–42 hours. Tags: options, option-contracts-and-structures.

Read call and put rights, premium units, expiry and maximum contractual exposure.

Hard prerequisites: C-B01. Recommended preparation: C-M02. Optional enrichment: none. Diagnostic: What can a short put require even after collecting its premium? Bridge: B01.

Estimation basis: Editorial estimate for selected readings, scope navigation, one worked calculation, one transfer variant, practical work and remediation; 45–90 minutes per ordinary scope topic plus 2–8 practical hours, longer for specialist derivation. Projects budgeted separately. Not measured learner time.

Objectives:

- Read call and put rights, premium units, expiry and maximum contractual exposure.
- Solve and explain: Long 100-strike call premium 3 multiplier 100 at expiry 110: payoff 1000, profit 700 before costs.
- Audit the failure mode: Confusing payoff with profit; assuming all multipliers are 100.

Mathematics: A (codes in MATH_ROADMAP). Programming: None for core explanations; optional Python for replication.

Reading: R01:II; R04:option basics. Resource verification limits control use; a TOC assignment is a candidate, not a reviewed chapter. All learners can complete the synthetic numerical case without buying a book.

Ordered scope:

- M21.01 — Calls: rights to buy, long/short obligations and premium
- M21.02 — Puts: rights to sell, long/short obligations and premium
- M21.03 — Opening and closing: buy-to-open, sell-to-open, buy-to-close, sell-to-close
- M21.04 — Contract specification: underlying, strike, expiry, multiplier, currency and deliverable
- M21.05 — Premium quotation: per-unit versus per-contract price, tick value and cash debit/credit
- M21.06 — Moneyness: ITM, ATM, OTM; spot versus forward moneyness
- M21.07 — Intrinsic value: exercise value versus economic value before expiry
- M21.08 — Extrinsic and time value: pricing inputs and limitations of shorthand decomposition
- M21.09 — Expiry terminology: expiration date, last trading day, exercise deadline and settlement date
- M21.10 — Exercise styles: American, European, Bermudan; geography is not the definition
- M21.11 — Settlement styles: cash settlement, physical settlement, delivery into futures
- M21.12 — Product families: equity, ETF, index and futures options
- M21.13 — Listing cycles: weeklies, monthlies, quarterlies, serials, LEAPS and daily expiries
- M21.14 — 0DTE options: remaining-time definition, intraday convexity and event concentration
- M21.15 — Adjusted contracts: nonstandard deliverables, fractional cash, changing multipliers
- M21.16 — Payoff versus profit: premium, financing, fees and pre-expiry mark-to-market
- M21.17 — FLEX and customized exchange-listed options: custom terms, exercise/settlement choices, liquidity and exact contract verification

Teaching brief for module delivery: begin with the checkpoint dilemma, elicit the learner's units and obligations, work the numerical case, then use the practical assignment to challenge the main assumption. Ask for a counterexample before extending the scope. This is a module delivery plan, not a topic lesson.

Practice:

**EX-M21-C** What can a short put require even after collecting its premium?

Reference: Use the module objective and stated failure mode; credit explicit conditions and a counterexample, not confidence.

**EX-M21-N** Compute, show units and assumptions; then change one input and justify the direction. Long 100-strike call premium 3 multiplier 100 at expiry 110: payoff 1000, profit 700 before costs.

Reference: Long 100-strike call premium 3 multiplier 100 at expiry 110: payoff 1000, profit 700 before costs.

**EX-M21-P** Decode unfamiliar live or archived contract specifications and construct basic payoff tables.

Reference: Get underlying, multiplier, units, exercise style and settlement correct on every case.

Common mistakes:

- Confusing payoff with profit; assuming all multipliers are 100.
- Accepting a result without its units, assumptions or source date.

Checkpoint questions:

- What can a short put require even after collecting its premium?
- Which assumption in your numerical result is most vulnerable, and what changes if it fails?

Mastery:

- Get underlying, multiplier, units, exercise style and settlement correct on every case.
- At least 85% on quiz and rubric; no unresolved critical errors; solve a fresh transfer variant with reasoning.

Remediation: Diagnose the mistaken unit, model, observation or cash-flow step; revisit the cited entry bridge or source section, annotate the correction, then attempt an unseen variant after a separate review session.
