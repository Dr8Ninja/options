# M66 — Failure taxonomy and historical case studies

Status: learning design; topic lessons remain P17. Slug: `failure-taxonomy-and-historical-case-studies`. Intermediate; 16–40 hours. Tags: failure-analysis, evidence-failure-analysis-and-professional-practice.

Reconstruct failure cases from dated evidence with competing explanations.

Hard prerequisites: C-M46, C-M49. Recommended preparation: C-M45, C-M55, C-M60. Optional enrichment: none. Diagnostic: What evidence would distinguish liquidity feedback from the initial shock? Bridge: B01.

Estimation basis: Editorial estimate for selected readings, scope navigation, one worked calculation, one transfer variant, practical work and remediation; 45–90 minutes per ordinary scope topic plus 2–8 practical hours, longer for specialist derivation. Projects budgeted separately. Not measured learner time.

Objectives:

- Reconstruct failure cases from dated evidence with competing explanations.
- Solve and explain: Leverage 20x: a 5% unhedged assetloss exhausts initialequity before costs under simple balance-sheet assumptions.
- Audit the failure mode: Single-cause stories and hindsight certainty.

Mathematics: A, S (codes in MATH_ROADMAP). Programming: None for core explanations; optional Python for replication.

Reading: R67:LTCM testimony; R68:pp 24–34; RX16:1987; RX17:2018. Resource verification limits control use; a TOC assignment is a candidate, not a reviewed chapter. All learners can complete the synthetic numerical case without buying a book.

Ordered scope:

- M66.01 — Knowledge failures: contract confusion, missing prerequisites and wrong probability model
- M66.02 — No-edge failures: weak rationale, cost-blindness, random results and mistaken risk premium
- M66.03 — Sizing failures: leverage, concentration, inconsistent risk and no margin buffer
- M66.04 — Tail failures: short convexity, negative skew, hidden correlation and gap risk
- M66.05 — Behavioral failures: revenge, FOMO, ego, rule overrides and concealment
- M66.06 — Research failures: leakage, survivorship, overfitting, cherry-picking and weak samples
- M66.07 — Execution failures: poor fills, illiquidity, failed stops, legging and excessive turnover
- M66.08 — Regime failures: strategy mismatch, structural breaks, crowding and delayed adaptation
- M66.09 — Operational failures: bad releases, missing controls, stale data and reconciliation gaps
- M66.10 — LTCM 1998 case: leverage, crowded relative value, funding and convergence risk
- M66.11 — Knight Capital 2012 case: software deployment, order controls and ignored warning evidence
- M66.12 — WTI April 2020 case: delivery constraints, expiring futures and nonpositive-price model assumptions
- M66.13 — 2021 equity/options episode: short interest, options activity, settlement demands and competing explanations
- M66.14 — Retail-performance studies: SEBI and Barber/Odean; population, period, fees and generalizability
- M66.15 — Short-volatility stress replays: 1987, 2008, February 2018, March 2020; source-specific investigation
- M66.16 — Incident postmortems: timeline, root causes, contributing factors, controls and counterfactual limits
- M66.P01 — Regulator report consistency audit: Reconcile R56 Table 5 against prose units and denominators; quarantine buyer/seller headline averages until corrected or raw data verified.

Teaching brief for module delivery: begin with the checkpoint dilemma, elicit the learner's units and obligations, work the numerical case, then use the practical assignment to challenge the main assumption. Ask for a counterexample before extending the scope. This is a module delivery plan, not a topic lesson.

Practice:

Reference: Use the module objective and stated failure mode; credit explicit conditions and a counterexample, not confidence.

**EX-M66-N** Compute, show units and assumptions; then change one input and justify the direction. Leverage 20x: a 5% unhedged assetloss exhausts initialequity before costs under simple balance-sheet assumptions.

Reference: Leverage 20x: a 5% unhedged assetloss exhausts initialequity before costs under simple balance-sheet assumptions.

**EX-M66-P** Reconstruct a documented loss event and map each failure to a control.

Reference: Distinguish known facts from hindsight and show which losses the control could realistically prevent.

Common mistakes:

- Single-cause stories and hindsight certainty.
- Accepting a result without its units, assumptions or source date.

Checkpoint questions:

- What evidence would distinguish liquidity feedback from the initial shock?
- Which assumption in your numerical result is most vulnerable, and what changes if it fails?

Mastery:

- Distinguish known facts from hindsight and show which losses the control could realistically prevent.
- At least 85% on quiz and rubric; no unresolved critical errors; solve a fresh transfer variant with reasoning.

Remediation: Diagnose the mistaken unit, model, observation or cash-flow step; revisit the cited entry bridge or source section, annotate the correction, then attempt an unseen variant after a separate review session.
