# M02 — Instruments and underlying-market structure

Status: learning design; topic lessons remain P17. Slug: `instruments-and-underlying-market-structure`. Beginner; 15–38 hours. Tags: market-mechanics, financial-and-market-foundations.

Choose the instrument whose ownership, liabilities and cash flows match the objective.

Hard prerequisites: C-M01. Recommended preparation: none. Optional enrichment: none. Diagnostic: How can an ETN fail when its reference index does not? Bridge: B01.

Estimation basis: Editorial estimate for selected readings, scope navigation, one worked calculation, one transfer variant, practical work and remediation; 45–90 minutes per ordinary scope topic plus 2–8 practical hours, longer for specialist derivation. Projects budgeted separately. Not measured learner time.

Objectives:

- Choose the instrument whose ownership, liabilities and cash flows match the objective.
- Solve and explain: An ETF moves +10% then -10%: 100 becomes 99; a hypothetical daily 2x product becomes 96 before costs.
- Audit the failure mode: Ignoring issuer credit and daily reset compounding.

Mathematics: A (codes in MATH_ROADMAP). Programming: None for core explanations; optional Python for replication.

Reading: R16:fixed-income and equity valuation lectures. Resource verification limits control use; a TOC assignment is a candidate, not a reviewed chapter. All learners can complete the synthetic numerical case without buying a book.

Ordered scope:

- M02.01 — Equities: ownership, common/preferred shares, capitalization, free float
- M02.02 — Bonds and bills: principal, coupons, yield, maturity, default risk
- M02.03 — Funds: mutual funds, ETFs, NAV, creation/redemption, tracking differences
- M02.04 — Exchange-traded notes: unsecured issuer exposure, acceleration and redemption provisions
- M02.05 — Leveraged and inverse funds: daily resets, path dependence, compounding and option implications
- M02.06 — Indices: price-weighted, capitalization-weighted, equal-weighted, price versus total return
- M02.07 — Index maintenance: rebalancing, constituent changes, divisor adjustments, closing flows
- M02.08 — Spot, forwards and futures: ownership versus contractual exposure, settlement obligations
- M02.09 — Options and warrants: contingent claims, issuer warrants versus exchange-cleared options
- M02.10 — Swaps: interest-rate, total-return, credit, variance and currency exposure
- M02.11 — Currencies: quotation direction, crosses, currency risk and cash balances
- M02.12 — Commodities: physical markets, inventories, storage, seasonality, delivery constraints
- M02.13 — Securities lending and repo: borrow availability, rebates, specialness, financing
- M02.14 — Structured products: embedded options, issuer credit, payoff decomposition
- M02.15 — Digital-asset derivatives: perpetuals, funding, collateral currency and venue risk

Teaching brief for module delivery: begin with the checkpoint dilemma, elicit the learner's units and obligations, work the numerical case, then use the practical assignment to challenge the main assumption. Ask for a counterexample before extending the scope. This is a module delivery plan, not a topic lesson.

Practice:

**EX-M02-C** How can an ETN fail when its reference index does not?

Reference: Use the module objective and stated failure mode; credit explicit conditions and a counterexample, not confidence.

**EX-M02-N** Compute, show units and assumptions; then change one input and justify the direction. An ETF moves +10% then -10%: 100 becomes 99; a hypothetical daily 2x product becomes 96 before costs.

Reference: An ETF moves +10% then -10%: 100 becomes 99; a hypothetical daily 2x product becomes 96 before costs.

**EX-M02-P** Compare a stock, ETF, leveraged ETF, future, option and swap expressing a similar thesis.

Reference: Identify ownership, leverage, path dependence, counterparty and settlement differences.

Common mistakes:

- Ignoring issuer credit and daily reset compounding.
- Accepting a result without its units, assumptions or source date.

Checkpoint questions:

- How can an ETN fail when its reference index does not?
- Which assumption in your numerical result is most vulnerable, and what changes if it fails?

Mastery:

- Identify ownership, leverage, path dependence, counterparty and settlement differences.
- At least 85% on quiz and rubric; no unresolved critical errors; solve a fresh transfer variant with reasoning.

Remediation: Diagnose the mistaken unit, model, observation or cash-flow step; revisit the cited entry bridge or source section, annotate the correction, then attempt an unseen variant after a separate review session.
