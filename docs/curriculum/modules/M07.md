# M07 — Macroeconomics and policy transmission

Status: learning design; topic lessons remain P17. Slug: `macroeconomics-and-policy-transmission`. Intermediate; 14–36 hours. Tags: macro, fundamentals-macro-and-events.

Separate economic news, release surprise and rate expectations in event hypotheses.

Hard prerequisites: C-M01. Recommended preparation: C-M02. Optional enrichment: none. Diagnostic: Why must an inflation backtest use the first published vintage? Bridge: B01.

Estimation basis: Editorial estimate for selected readings, scope navigation, one worked calculation, one transfer variant, practical work and remediation; 45–90 minutes per ordinary scope topic plus 2–8 practical hours, longer for specialist derivation. Projects budgeted separately. Not measured learner time.

Objectives:

- Separate economic news, release surprise and rate expectations in event hypotheses.
- Solve and explain: Expected CPI3.0%, released 3.2%: surprise 0.2 percentage points, not 0.2% relative.
- Audit the failure mode: Using revised data or knowing releases before timestamps.

Mathematics: A, S (codes in MATH_ROADMAP). Programming: None for core explanations; optional Python for replication.

Reading: R18:real-time periods; R19:release definitions. Resource verification limits control use; a TOC assignment is a candidate, not a reviewed chapter. All learners can complete the synthetic numerical case without buying a book.

Ordered scope:

- M07.01 — Economic activity: GDP, growth, recession, output gaps, revisions
- M07.02 — Inflation: CPI, PPI, PCE, headline/core, base effects and seasonal adjustments
- M07.03 — Labor markets: payrolls, unemployment, wages, participation, claims
- M07.04 — Central banks: policy rates, reaction functions, mandates, communication
- M07.05 — Monetary policy: quantitative easing/tightening, reserves, repo and transmission channels
- M07.06 — Fiscal policy: deficits, issuance, spending, taxes and policy uncertainty
- M07.07 — Yield curves: spot, par and forward curves; steepening, flattening, inversion
- M07.08 — Real rates and breakevens: nominal/real yield decomposition and inflation risk premium
- M07.09 — Credit spreads: default expectations, recovery, liquidity, risk aversion
- M07.10 — Economic calendars: release timing, consensus, revisions, surprise and positioning
- M07.11 — Market-implied policy paths: futures, OIS, term premium and probability assumptions
- M07.12 — Liquidity narratives: monetary, funding and market liquidity; avoid conflating them
- M07.13 — Regime frameworks: growth/inflation combinations, supply versus demand shocks
- M07.14 — Causal humility: simultaneous news, anticipation, endogeneity and hindsight stories

Teaching brief for module delivery: begin with the checkpoint dilemma, elicit the learner's units and obligations, work the numerical case, then use the practical assignment to challenge the main assumption. Ask for a counterexample before extending the scope. This is a module delivery plan, not a topic lesson.

Practice:

Reference: Distinguish what was knowable at the decision time from hindsight.

Common mistakes:

- Using revised data or knowing releases before timestamps.
- Accepting a result without its units, assumptions or source date.

Checkpoint questions:

- Why must an inflation backtest use the first published vintage?
- Which assumption in your numerical result is most vulnerable, and what changes if it fails?

Mastery:

- Distinguish what was knowable at the decision time from hindsight.
- At least 85% on quiz and rubric; no unresolved critical errors; solve a fresh transfer variant with reasoning.

Remediation: Diagnose the mistaken unit, model, observation or cash-flow step; revisit the cited entry bridge or source section, annotate the correction, then attempt an unseen variant after a separate review session.
