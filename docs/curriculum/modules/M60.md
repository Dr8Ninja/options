# M60 — Psychology, decision hygiene and deliberate practice

Status: learning design; topic lessons remain P17. Slug: `psychology-decision-hygiene-and-deliberate-practice`. Beginner; 22–52 hours. Tags: psychology, decision-practice-and-professional-development.

Recognize behavioral errors and evaluate decision quality separately from returns.

Hard prerequisites: C-B01, C-M10. Recommended preparation: C-M01, C-M45. Optional enrichment: none. Diagnostic: Can a losing trade be a good decision under a sound distribution? Bridge: B01.

Estimation basis: Editorial estimate for selected readings, scope navigation, one worked calculation, one transfer variant, practical work and remediation; 45–90 minutes per ordinary scope topic plus 2–8 practical hours, longer for specialist derivation. Projects budgeted separately. Not measured learner time.

Objectives:

- Recognize behavioral errors and evaluate decision quality separately from returns.
- Solve and explain: A fair coin producing 8 heads in 10 trials has probability 45/1024 for exactly 8; not proof of skill.
- Audit the failure mode: Revenge trading, FOMO, anchoring, loss aversion and outcome bias.

Mathematics: A, S (codes in MATH_ROADMAP). Programming: None for core explanations; optional Python for replication.

Reading: R65:lecture transcript; R64:sample-limited empirical study. Resource verification limits control use; a TOC assignment is a candidate, not a reviewed chapter. All learners can complete the synthetic numerical case without buying a book.

Ordered scope:

- M60.01 — Fear, greed and hope: emotional state, distorted choices and observable behavior
- M60.02 — Loss aversion: reference points, asymmetric response and risk seeking after losses
- M60.03 — Anchoring: entry price, prior highs, forecasts and arbitrary targets
- M60.04 — Overconfidence: calibration, illusion of knowledge and excessive trading
- M60.05 — Confirmation and availability bias: selective evidence, salient stories and source choice
- M60.06 — Recency bias: extrapolating streaks and regimes from small samples
- M60.07 — Outcome and hindsight bias: judging process from one result and rewriting expectations
- M60.08 — Sunk-cost fallacy and disposition effect: holding losers, selling winners and roll rationalization
- M60.09 — Gambler's fallacy and illusion of control: independent events, patterns and causal confusion
- M60.10 — FOMO and revenge trading: triggers, escalating size and precommitted interruption rules
- M60.11 — Hesitation and performance anxiety: uncertain action, missed trades and exposure pressure
- M60.12 — Ego and identity attachment: defending a thesis, public predictions and loss concealment
- M60.13 — Boredom and overtrading: stimulation, unnecessary activity and opportunity filtering
- M60.14 — Addiction-like behavior: loss of control, borrowing to trade, impaired life function and help-seeking
- M60.15 — Probabilistic thinking: forecasts, confidence levels, calibration and updating
- M60.16 — Process versus outcome: ex-ante decision record, luck and repeated performance
- M60.17 — Emotional regulation: pauses, routines, sleep, workload and stress awareness
- M60.18 — Pre-trade checklists: decision-critical fields and behavioral commitments
- M60.19 — Post-trade review: error taxonomy, counterfactuals and focused improvement
- M60.20 — Deliberate practice: replay, feedback, progressively difficult tasks and spaced retrieval
- M60.21 — Winning/losing streak protocols: stable limits, no entitlement and reassessment thresholds
- M60.22 — Decision hygiene: independent estimates, premortems, dissent and documented overrides

Teaching brief for module delivery: begin with the checkpoint dilemma, elicit the learner's units and obligations, work the numerical case, then use the practical assignment to challenge the main assumption. Ask for a counterexample before extending the scope. This is a module delivery plan, not a topic lesson.

Common mistakes:

- Revenge trading, FOMO, anchoring, loss aversion and outcome bias.
- Accepting a result without its units, assumptions or source date.

Checkpoint questions:

- Can a losing trade be a good decision under a sound distribution?
- Which assumption in your numerical result is most vulnerable, and what changes if it fails?

Mastery:

- Identify behavior changes, follow pause rules and judge decisions from ex-ante information.
- At least 85% on quiz and rubric; no unresolved critical errors; solve a fresh transfer variant with reasoning.
