# M59 — Trading styles and complete system design

Status: learning design; topic lessons remain P17. Slug: `trading-styles-and-complete-system-design`. Intermediate; 17–42 hours. Tags: trading-systems, decision-practice-and-professional-development.

Turn a thesis into explicit states, risk limits and decision records.

Hard prerequisites: C-M53, C-M55. Recommended preparation: C-M23, C-M45. Optional enrichment: none. Diagnostic: What is the rule when data quality or execution state becomes unknown? Bridge: B01.

Estimation basis: Editorial estimate for selected readings, scope navigation, one worked calculation, one transfer variant, practical work and remediation; 45–90 minutes per ordinary scope topic plus 2–8 practical hours, longer for specialist derivation. Projects budgeted separately. Not measured learner time.

Objectives:

- Turn a thesis into explicit states, risk limits and decision records.
- Solve and explain: If daily simulated loss limit 200 is reached at 205, flag breach, stop new risk and reconcile; do not reset ledger.
- Audit the failure mode: Vague discretionary overrides disguised as rules.

Mathematics: A, S (codes in MATH_ROADMAP). Programming: None for core explanations; optional Python for replication.

Reading: R48:contingency planning; R23:research validity. Resource verification limits control use; a TOC assignment is a candidate, not a reviewed chapter. All learners can complete the synthetic numerical case without buying a book.

Ordered scope:

- M59.01 — Scalping: small margins, frequency, latency, costs and adverse selection
- M59.02 — Intraday trading: session structure, news, liquidity and overnight boundary
- M59.03 — Swing trading: multi-session holds, gaps, event calendar and time decay
- M59.04 — Position trading: longer thesis, capital usage, changing fundamentals and carry
- M59.05 — Event trading: discrete distributions, calendar precision and repricing
- M59.06 — Trend and momentum trading: persistence, whipsaw, tails and exit policy
- M59.07 — Mean-reversion trading: equilibrium hypothesis, structural breaks and adverse trends
- M59.08 — Breakout trading: volatility transition, false breaks and execution cost
- M59.09 — Premium buying and selling: purchased convexity versus sold risk, no automatic edge
- M59.10 — Systematic versus discretionary: explicit rules, judgment logs, hybrids and accountability
- M59.11 — Market making: spread capture, inventory, information disadvantage and infrastructure
- M59.12 — Arbitrage and relative value: replication versus convergence speculation and funding
- M59.13 — System specification: market, regime, setup, signal, entry, confirmation and invalidation
- M59.14 — Options implementation: instrument, strike, expiry, Greeks, volatility and event constraints
- M59.15 — Risk and execution rules: size, stop, target, adjustment, exit, order type and max exposure
- M59.16 — Governance rules: daily/weekly loss limits, drawdown, review, suspension and retirement
- M59.17 — Specialization choice: data, capital, time, temperament, access and realistic capacity

Teaching brief for module delivery: begin with the checkpoint dilemma, elicit the learner's units and obligations, work the numerical case, then use the practical assignment to challenge the main assumption. Ask for a counterexample before extending the scope. This is a module delivery plan, not a topic lesson.

Reference: Use the module objective and stated failure mode; credit explicit conditions and a counterexample, not confidence.

Reference: If daily simulated loss limit 200 is reached at 205, flag breach, stop new risk and reconcile; do not reset ledger.

**EX-M59-P** Write a complete one-market trading-system specification.

Reference: Another person must be able to reproduce every decision and identify when trading is prohibited.

Common mistakes:

- Vague discretionary overrides disguised as rules.
- Accepting a result without its units, assumptions or source date.

Checkpoint questions:

- What is the rule when data quality or execution state becomes unknown?
- Which assumption in your numerical result is most vulnerable, and what changes if it fails?

Mastery:

- Another person must be able to reproduce every decision and identify when trading is prohibited.
- At least 85% on quiz and rubric; no unresolved critical errors; solve a fresh transfer variant with reasoning.

Remediation: Diagnose the mistaken unit, model, observation or cash-flow step; revisit the cited entry bridge or source section, annotate the correction, then attempt an unseen variant after a separate review session.
