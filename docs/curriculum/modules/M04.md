# M04 — Quotes, orders and matching

Status: learning design; topic lessons remain P17. Slug: `quotes-orders-and-matching`. Beginner; 15–38 hours. Tags: execution, financial-and-market-foundations.

Read a quote and select an order with explicit price, fill and cancellation risks.

Hard prerequisites: C-M21. Recommended preparation: C-M03. Optional enrichment: none. Diagnostic: Can a stop guarantee an exit price during a gap? Bridge: B01.

Estimation basis: Editorial estimate for selected readings, scope navigation, one worked calculation, one transfer variant, practical work and remediation; 45–90 minutes per ordinary scope topic plus 2–8 practical hours, longer for specialist derivation. Projects budgeted separately. Not measured learner time.

Objectives:

- Read a quote and select an order with explicit price, fill and cancellation risks.
- Solve and explain: Bid 2.00 ask 2.20, multiplier 100: immediate buy then sell loses 20 before fees.
- Audit the failure mode: Treating displayed mid or queue position as a guaranteed fill.

Mathematics: A (codes in MATH_ROADMAP). Programming: None for core explanations; optional Python for replication.

Reading: R30:complex order handling, reading pending access. Resource verification limits control use; a TOC assignment is a candidate, not a reviewed chapter. All learners can complete the synthetic numerical case without buying a book.

Ordered scope:

- M04.01 — Bid and ask: best quotes, midpoint, spread, quoted versus executable size
- M04.02 — Last trade and mark: stale prints, valuation conventions, non-executable marks
- M04.03 — Order books: price levels, depth, top-of-book versus full-depth feeds
- M04.04 — Market orders: immediacy, uncertain fill price, fragmented executions
- M04.05 — Limit orders: price control, non-execution risk, adverse selection
- M04.06 — Stop orders: trigger rules, conversion to market orders, gap risk
- M04.07 — Stop-limit orders: trigger and limit, non-fill risk after a gap
- M04.08 — Time-in-force: day, GTC, IOC, FOK, expiry and session restrictions
- M04.09 — Conditional orders: OCO, brackets, trailing stops, broker-side versus exchange-side handling
- M04.10 — Matching priority: price-time, pro rata, size priority, allocation exceptions
- M04.11 — Partial fills: remaining quantity, average fill, cancel/replace, race conditions
- M04.12 — Auctions: opening, closing, volatility and options price-improvement auctions
- M04.13 — Tick sizes: minimum increments, spread constraints, rounding and price discretion
- M04.14 — Complex orders: package net prices, ratios, leg-level fills, complex order books
- M04.15 — Order errors: wrong side, wrong expiry, wrong multiplier, duplicate submissions

Teaching brief for module delivery: begin with the checkpoint dilemma, elicit the learner's units and obligations, work the numerical case, then use the practical assignment to challenge the main assumption. Ask for a counterexample before extending the scope. This is a module delivery plan, not a topic lesson.

Practice:

**EX-M04-C** Can a stop guarantee an exit price during a gap?

Reference: Use the module objective and stated failure mode; credit explicit conditions and a counterexample, not confidence.

**EX-M04-N** Compute, show units and assumptions; then change one input and justify the direction. Bid 2.00 ask 2.20, multiplier 100: immediate buy then sell loses 20 before fees.

Reference: Bid 2.00 ask 2.20, multiplier 100: immediate buy then sell loses 20 before fees.

**EX-M04-P** Replay market, limit, stop and stop-limit orders through a gapping order book.

Reference: Explain fills, non-fills, partial fills and why a stop does not guarantee an exit price.

Common mistakes:

- Treating displayed mid or queue position as a guaranteed fill.
- Accepting a result without its units, assumptions or source date.

Checkpoint questions:

- Can a stop guarantee an exit price during a gap?
- Which assumption in your numerical result is most vulnerable, and what changes if it fails?

Mastery:

- Explain fills, non-fills, partial fills and why a stop does not guarantee an exit price.
- At least 85% on quiz and rubric; no unresolved critical errors; solve a fresh transfer variant with reasoning.

Remediation: Diagnose the mistaken unit, model, observation or cash-flow step; revisit the cited entry bridge or source section, annotate the correction, then attempt an unseen variant after a separate review session.
