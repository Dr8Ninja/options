# M46 — Scenario analysis, stress and tail risk

Status: learning design; topic lessons remain P17. Slug: `scenario-analysis-stress-and-tail-risk`. Intermediate; 17–42 hours. Tags: risk, risk-and-portfolio-construction.

Use VaR, ES and scenario loss with stated horizon and liquidity assumptions.

Hard prerequisites: C-M45, C-M11. Recommended preparation: C-M32, C-M36. Optional enrichment: none. Diagnostic: What losses can a 99% VaR number conceal? Bridge: B02.

Estimation basis: Editorial estimate for selected readings, scope navigation, one worked calculation, one transfer variant, practical work and remediation; 45–90 minutes per ordinary scope topic plus 2–8 practical hours, longer for specialist derivation. Projects budgeted separately. Not measured learner time.

Objectives:

- Use VaR, ES and scenario loss with stated horizon and liquidity assumptions.
- Solve and explain: Losses 0,1,2,3,100: worst 20% empirical tailmean 100 under a stated discrete convention.
- Audit the failure mode: Treating VaR as worst loss; excluding stressed correlations.

Mathematics: S, L, N (codes in MATH_ROADMAP). Programming: None for core explanations; optional Python for replication.

Reading: R44:publisher catalog metadata only; R45:scope and supersession. Resource verification limits control use; a TOC assignment is a candidate, not a reviewed chapter. All learners can complete the synthetic numerical case without buying a book.

Ordered scope:

- M46.01 — Scenario grids: joint spot, volatility, time, rates and dividend shocks
- M46.02 — Full revaluation: nonlinear positions and limitations of local Greeks
- M46.03 — Historical stress tests: event selection, mapping exposures and regime differences
- M46.04 — Hypothetical stress tests: unseen combinations, overnight gaps and broken hedges
- M46.05 — Reverse stress testing: conditions that breach capital, margin or liquidity limits
- M46.06 — Value at Risk: horizon, confidence, parametric/historical/Monte Carlo methods
- M46.07 — Expected shortfall/CVaR: conditional tail losses, coherence and estimation uncertainty
- M46.08 — Extreme-value methods: peaks over threshold, tail index and limited data
- M46.09 — Tail dependence and copulas: joint extremes, correlation inadequacy and model risk
- M46.10 — Liquidity stress: spread widening, disappearing depth and inability to unwind all legs
- M46.11 — Margin stress: changing requirements, collateral haircuts and forced liquidation
- M46.12 — Jump and overnight risk: discontinuities, stop gaps and unavailable hedge venues
- M46.13 — Volatility surface stress: level, skew, curvature, term structure and vol-of-vol
- M46.14 — Stress path dependence: sequence of moves, cash calls and interim failure before recovery
- M46.15 — Hedge breakdown: basis, counterparty, borrow recall and correlation changes
- M46.16 — Stress report design: worst scenario, capital needs, residual risks and action thresholds
- M46.17 — Jump-to-default and discontinuous recovery: single-name option risk, residual stock/futures, hedge availability and settlement

Teaching brief for module delivery: begin with the checkpoint dilemma, elicit the learner's units and obligations, work the numerical case, then use the practical assignment to challenge the main assumption. Ask for a counterexample before extending the scope. This is a module delivery plan, not a topic lesson.

Common mistakes:

- Treating VaR as worst loss; excluding stressed correlations.
- Accepting a result without its units, assumptions or source date.

Checkpoint questions:

- What losses can a 99% VaR number conceal?
- Which assumption in your numerical result is most vulnerable, and what changes if it fails?

Mastery:

- Identify ruin paths and funding failure even when final terminal payoff seems acceptable.
- At least 85% on quiz and rubric; no unresolved critical errors; solve a fresh transfer variant with reasoning.

Remediation: Diagnose the mistaken unit, model, observation or cash-flow step; revisit the cited entry bridge or source section, annotate the correction, then attempt an unseen variant after a separate review session.
