# M65 — Retail myths and evidence standards

Status: learning design; topic lessons remain P17. Slug: `retail-myths-and-evidence-standards`. Intermediate; 20–48 hours. Tags: evidence-and-myths, evidence-failure-analysis-and-professional-practice.

Classify conditional theory, empirical evidence, heuristics and unsupported narratives.

Hard prerequisites: C-M11, C-M21. Recommended preparation: C-M10, C-M45. Optional enrichment: none. Diagnostic: Why is a plausible mechanism insufficient evidence of a profitable edge? Bridge: B01.

Estimation basis: Editorial estimate for selected readings, scope navigation, one worked calculation, one transfer variant, practical work and remediation; 45–90 minutes per ordinary scope topic plus 2–8 practical hours, longer for specialist derivation. Projects budgeted separately. Not measured learner time.

Objectives:

- Classify conditional theory, empirical evidence, heuristics and unsupported narratives.
- Solve and explain: A backtest with 100% winning observations and one omitted loss has unknown true result until omission repaired.
- Audit the failure mode: Appeals to reputation, marketing, snippets or false precision.

Mathematics: A, S (codes in MATH_ROADMAP). Programming: None for core explanations; optional Python for replication.

Reading: R23:selection validity; R68:gamma-squeeze finding. Resource verification limits control use; a TOC assignment is a candidate, not a reviewed chapter. All learners can complete the synthetic numerical case without buying a book.

Ordered scope:

- M65.01 — “90% of options expire worthless”: denominator, closed/exercised/expired contracts and unsupported universal percentage
- M65.02 — “Sellers always have the edge”: risk premium, costs, adverse selection and regime-dependent compensation
- M65.03 — “Theta guarantees profit”: gamma, vega, jumps, financing and tail losses
- M65.04 — “High probability means a good trade”: payoff magnitude, costs, capital and estimation uncertainty
- M65.05 — “A 70% win rate proves profitability”: average wins/losses, rare tails and full distribution
- M65.06 — “RSI above 70 means sell”: persistent trends, indicator definition and missing validation
- M65.07 — “Max pain predicts expiry”: descriptive statistic, causal claim and evidence burden
- M65.08 — “Large OI guarantees support/resistance”: unknown position signs, hedges and changing inventory
- M65.09 — “More indicators improve accuracy”: redundancy, multiple testing and diminishing incremental information
- M65.10 — “Option buying is gambling”: purpose, price, forecast and sizing determine the economic case
- M65.11 — “Option selling is safer”: collateral, tail loss, margin and negative skew
- M65.12 — “Technical analysis always works”: conditional evidence, definitions, costs and sample dependence
- M65.13 — “Price action alone is enough”: missing volatility, contracts, execution and portfolio constraints
- M65.14 — “Delta is the chance of profit”: pricing convention, expiry ITM probability and profit are different
- M65.15 — “IV rank measures cheapness”: historical context versus forecast value and tail pricing
- M65.16 — “A roll for credit erases a loss”: accounting, new obligations and cumulative capital risk
- M65.17 — “Defined risk means no operational danger”: expiry mismatch, assignment, legging and fees
- M65.18 — “Dealer gamma tells you tomorrow's direction”: inferred inventory, model assumptions and causal limits
- M65.19 — “Screenshots prove an edge”: omitted losses, deposits, selection bias and unverifiable execution
- M65.20 — “Arbitrage is always risk-free in practice”: theoretical conditions, funding, timing, settlement and access

Teaching brief for module delivery: begin with the checkpoint dilemma, elicit the learner's units and obligations, work the numerical case, then use the practical assignment to challenge the main assumption. Ask for a counterexample before extending the scope. This is a module delivery plan, not a topic lesson.

Practice:

**EX-M65-C** Why is a plausible mechanism insufficient evidence of a profitable edge?

Reference: Use the module objective and stated failure mode; credit explicit conditions and a counterexample, not confidence.

**EX-M65-N** Compute, show units and assumptions; then change one input and justify the direction. A backtest with 100% winning observations and one omitted loss has unknown true result until omission repaired.

Reference: A backtest with 100% winning observations and one omitted loss has unknown true result until omission repaired.

**EX-M65-P** Write an evidence verdict for each listed myth with a counterexample or test design.

Reference: Distinguish false, overstated, unsupported and context-dependent claims without replacing one myth with another.

Common mistakes:

- Appeals to reputation, marketing, snippets or false precision.
- Accepting a result without its units, assumptions or source date.

Checkpoint questions:

- Why is a plausible mechanism insufficient evidence of a profitable edge?
- Which assumption in your numerical result is most vulnerable, and what changes if it fails?

Mastery:

- Distinguish false, overstated, unsupported and context-dependent claims without replacing one myth with another.
- At least 85% on quiz and rubric; no unresolved critical errors; solve a fresh transfer variant with reasoning.

Remediation: Diagnose the mistaken unit, model, observation or cash-flow step; revisit the cited entry bridge or source section, annotate the correction, then attempt an unseen variant after a separate review session.
