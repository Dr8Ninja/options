# M11 — Statistical inference and scientific reasoning

Status: learning design; topic lessons remain P17. Slug: `statistical-inference-and-scientific-reasoning`. Intermediate; 14–36 hours. Tags: quantitative, mathematics-and-inference.

Report uncertainty and make statistical decisions without claiming certainty.

Hard prerequisites: C-M10. Recommended preparation: none. Optional enrichment: none. Diagnostic: Why does selecting the best of many trials invalidate an ordinary single-test p-value? Bridge: B01.

Estimation basis: Editorial estimate for selected readings, scope navigation, one worked calculation, one transfer variant, practical work and remediation; 45–90 minutes per ordinary scope topic plus 2–8 practical hours, longer for specialist derivation. Projects budgeted separately. Not measured learner time.

Objectives:

- Report uncertainty and make statistical decisions without claiming certainty.
- Solve and explain: 100 independent fair Bernoulli trials: standard error of mean .05; dependence invalidates this estimate.
- Audit the failure mode: Multiple testing, selection bias and outcome-conditioned hypotheses.

Mathematics: S (codes in MATH_ROADMAP). Programming: None for core explanations; optional Python for replication.

Reading: R21:confidence intervals and tests; R23:sections 1–3. Resource verification limits control use; a TOC assignment is a candidate, not a reviewed chapter. All learners can complete the synthetic numerical case without buying a book.

Ordered scope:

- M11.01 — Population and sample: sampling process, data-generating process, representativeness
- M11.02 — Estimation: bias, consistency, efficiency, standard error and uncertainty
- M11.03 — Confidence intervals: coverage, dependence assumptions, interval interpretation
- M11.04 — Hypothesis testing: null, alternative, p-values, effect size, power and errors
- M11.05 — Statistical versus economic significance: tradability after costs and constraints
- M11.06 — Regression: simple/multiple linear models, residuals, coefficient interpretation
- M11.07 — Regression diagnostics: heteroskedasticity, autocorrelation, nonlinearity, multicollinearity
- M11.08 — Robust estimates: medians, trimmed statistics, outliers versus real tail events
- M11.09 — Bootstrap: resampling, confidence intervals, block bootstrap for dependent returns
- M11.10 — Bayesian estimation: shrinkage, posterior predictive checks, model uncertainty
- M11.11 — Multiple testing: family-wise errors, false discovery rate, researcher degrees of freedom
- M11.12 — Bias taxonomy: sampling, selection, survivorship, publication and confirmation bias
- M11.13 — Causal inference: experiments, confounding, instrumental variables, natural experiments
- M11.14 — Research replication: registered hypotheses, reproducible datasets, negative results

Teaching brief for module delivery: begin with the checkpoint dilemma, elicit the learner's units and obligations, work the numerical case, then use the practical assignment to challenge the main assumption. Ask for a counterexample before extending the scope. This is a module delivery plan, not a topic lesson.

Practice:

**EX-M11-C** Why does selecting the best of many trials invalidate an ordinary single-test p-value?

Reference: Use the module objective and stated failure mode; credit explicit conditions and a counterexample, not confidence.

**EX-M11-N** Compute, show units and assumptions; then change one input and justify the direction. 100 independent fair Bernoulli trials: standard error of mean .05; dependence invalidates this estimate.

Reference: 100 independent fair Bernoulli trials: standard error of mean .05; dependence invalidates this estimate.

**EX-M11-P** Estimate an edge with uncertainty, then repeat after multiple-testing correction.

Reference: Explain effect size, sample dependence, power and why a small p-value is insufficient.

Common mistakes:

- Multiple testing, selection bias and outcome-conditioned hypotheses.
- Accepting a result without its units, assumptions or source date.

Checkpoint questions:

- Why does selecting the best of many trials invalidate an ordinary single-test p-value?
- Which assumption in your numerical result is most vulnerable, and what changes if it fails?

Mastery:

- Explain effect size, sample dependence, power and why a small p-value is insufficient.
- At least 85% on quiz and rubric; no unresolved critical errors; solve a fresh transfer variant with reasoning.

Remediation: Diagnose the mistaken unit, model, observation or cash-flow step; revisit the cited entry bridge or source section, annotate the correction, then attempt an unseen variant after a separate review session.
